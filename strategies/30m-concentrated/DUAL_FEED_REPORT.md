# Dual-Feed Strategy Backtest Report

**Date:** 2026-04-13
**Data:** Coinbase + Hyperliquid 30m candles, TEST split (Jul–Dec 2025, overlapping bars only)
**Branch:** autotrader/30m-exp1

## Motivation

CB Live and HL Paper run the same strategy on different exchange price feeds. Over 112 live trades, they disagreed on direction 21.6% of the time. HL Paper was outperforming CB Live.

**Hypothesis:** Combining signals from both feeds could filter out noisy conflicting signals and produce higher-conviction entries.

**Three variants tested:**
- **A: Consensus** — require both feeds to independently confirm (MIN_VOTES met on both + HTF on both)
- **B: 10-Vote Pool** — pool 5 indicators from each feed into 10 total votes, require 7/10 or 6/10
- **C: HL Signal, CB Execution** — use HL prices for all signal generation, execute on CB

## Analysis

### None beat the baseline.

The single-feed CB baseline (Sharpe 26.11, $70M) outperforms every dual-feed variant. The same compounding dynamic from the fee-reduction analysis applies: fewer trades = less compounding = less total return, even when per-trade quality is comparable or slightly better.

### Option C (HL Signal) is the most interesting near-miss

- **Best win rate** (73.5% vs 73.2% baseline) and **best profit factor** (8.53 vs 8.32) of any variant tested
- Only 104 fewer trades than baseline
- Lost just $3.4M vs baseline — the smallest gap

This confirms the hypothesis that HL produces marginally better signals for this strategy — but the improvement is not large enough to overcome the slight trade count reduction. The signals generated by HL that CB would have missed are roughly balanced by CB signals that HL wouldn't have generated.

### Consensus (Option A) over-filters

Requiring both feeds to agree drops 566 trades and $12.9M in equity. The 22% directional disagreement between feeds isn't noise to be filtered — it's each exchange seeing slightly different price action that still contains valid signal. Filtering it out removes real alpha.

### 10-Vote Pool: threshold matters enormously

- **7/10**: Catastrophic. Sharpe dropped to 22.11, equity to $21.3M. The 70% threshold is too restrictive and kills trade frequency.
- **6/10**: Nearly matches baseline (Sharpe 25.87, -$6.3M). A 60% threshold across 10 votes is roughly equivalent to the original 60% threshold (3/5) on a single feed, so this mostly recreates the baseline with extra computation.

### Why dual-feed doesn't help

The core insight: **the directional disagreement between exchanges is not noise — it's signal.**

When HL says "long" and CB says "flat," the HL signal reflects real momentum on HL's order book that CB hasn't seen yet (or vice versa). Filtering these moments removes entries that would have been profitable. The 49% live agreement rate (and 78% backtest overlap) doesn't mean the disagreeing signals are wrong — they're responding to real but exchange-specific price dynamics.

## Conclusion

**Stay on single-feed CB baseline.** The dual-feed approach adds complexity without improving returns. The strategy's edge comes from high trade frequency and compounding, and any filter that reduces trade count hurts more than it helps.

The only scenario where dual-feed becomes interesting: if we could use the **disagreement itself** as a signal (e.g., when HL is strongly bullish but CB is flat, that predicts CB will catch up). This is a more sophisticated cross-exchange arbitrage thesis — different from what we tested here.
